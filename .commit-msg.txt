unbonded-offload probe: a local teardown is inconclusive, not a strap refusal (#1804)

The probe was enabled on a WHOOP 5/MG — the first time it ran on a strap in the
state it was written for. It made three attempts, concluded the strap refuses
the offload unbonded, and permanently retired itself. That conclusion is not
supported by the evidence.

The three probe attempts ended at 10776/10761/10787 ms — a 30 ms spread that is
a timer, not a radio event — with `status=22` (GATT_CONN_TERMINATE_LOCAL_HOST)
and `via=unknown` on every one. The link was terminated LOCALLY, by our own
stack, not by the strap. No ATT errors, no refusals, no callbacks — the four
subscribes simply never came back before the local hang-up.

The probe counted each local teardown as a strap verdict, charged it to the
silence budget, and latched `previouslyRefused` after three. A strap that holds
links for hours without the probe was being retired on the basis of links OUR
side tore down.

The fix classifies the disconnect cause before charging the budget:

- `unbondedProbeLinkLostIsLocalTeardown(status, origin)` — pure, unit-tested.
  Returns true for status=22 (GATT_CONN_TERMINATE_LOCAL_HOST), false for
  everything else (timeout, peer-user, etc.).
- In the disconnect handler, a local teardown gets
  `unbondedProbeLinkLostLocalTeardownLine` (inconclusive, does NOT charge the
  budget) instead of `unbondedProbeLinkLostLine`/`unbondedProbeLinkLostAskingLine`
  (strap-side drop, DOES charge the budget).
- The existing strap-side-drop lines and their budget charges are unchanged.

The probe will now retry on a local teardown rather than retiring, which is the
correct behaviour: the question was never asked of the strap. Only an ATT error
— an insufficient-authentication status above all — is an answer.

The `via=unknown` gap (NOOP knows it terminated the link and cannot say which
path did it) is named by the new line but not resolved by this change — finding
the ~10.8 s local teardown path is a separate investigation.
